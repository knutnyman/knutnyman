Why the gap? Pure selection bias. Trials enrol patients above an A1c threshold (commonly ≥7.5%), so their baselines overstate the true population mean. Never quote the 8.6% trial baseline as the population average — state which population you mean.

The treatment gradient — % reaching A1c target

The lighter the therapy, the better the control — diet > oral > insulin. (Partly confounded by indication: insulin is started in longer / more severe disease, so this is not a causal effect of therapy.)

⚠ Common miscitation: MOBILE (JAMA 2021, baseline 9.1%) and the DIAMOND T2D cohort are basal/MDI-insulin-treated INSULIN — not NIT. Exclude them from NIT figures despite their frequent appearance in "CGM in T2D" searches.

Recommended phrasing

Honest population statement "The average non-insulin-treated T2D patient has an HbA1c of roughly 7.2% (55 mmol/mol), versus ~8.6% (70 mmol/mol) for insulin-treated patients."
Device / market deck "Inadequately-controlled non-insulin T2D — the CGM target population — has a baseline A1c of ~8.6–8.8% (IMMEDIATE, CONNECT); CGM lowers A1c by ~0.3–0.9% vs control."

Caveats — read before quoting any number

  • Two populations, two numbers — never quote the 8.6% trial baseline as the population average.
  • Selection bias inflates every trial baseline (entry floors of A1c ≥7.1–7.5%).
  • "Non-insulin" is heterogeneous — diet-only (~6.5–7.0%) < metformin-only < multi-oral/GLP-1.
  • Confounding by indication drives the NIT-vs-insulin gap (disease stage, not therapy).
  • MOBILE / DIAMOND-T2D are insulin-treated — exclude from NIT figures.
  • CONNECT is press-sourced (ADA 2026); reconfirm exact figures vs the peer-reviewed manuscript.
  • No clean NIT-specific A1c distribution (NHANES-style %<7/7–8/8–9/>9) was verifiable; the population mean is a reasoned synthesis, not a single published figure.
