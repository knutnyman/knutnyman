# Business Plan — Nordic Protein Drink Co. (working name: **"VÄLT"**)
### A ready-to-drink protein milkshake brand built to compete with Propud (NJIE)

> *Working document. All financial figures are good-faith estimates for planning, not audited quotes. Validate every cost with real co-packer and supplier quotes before committing capital. Currency: SEK, with EUR references (≈ 11.4 SEK/EUR).*

> **📍 Going to the US?** The original plan (Sections 1–11) targets Sweden. The US is a much larger but far more competitive market with different formats, costs, and rules. See **[Part B — United States Edition](#part-b--united-states-edition)** for the full US-adapted strategy, USD cost-per-bottle model, and an honest read on what it takes to win there.

## 1. Executive summary

**The opportunity.** Propud (NJIE) created and now dominates the Swedish ready-to-drink (RTD) protein milkshake category: a 330 ml lactose-free, no-added-sugar shake with ~20 g protein, sold chilled in grocery (ICA, Coop, Willys) and convenience. The category is growing double-digits, riding a structural consumer shift toward high-protein, low-sugar "functional snacking." Propud proved the format works; the category is now big enough to support a strong #2 or a differentiated challenger.

**Our play.** Launch a 330 ml RTD protein shake that matches Propud on the table-stakes (20–25 g protein, lactose-free, no added sugar, great taste, chilled grocery distribution) but wins on **three differentiators**:

1. **Clean-label sweetening** — stevia/monk fruit only, *no aspartame or acesulfame-K* (Propud uses both). This is the single clearest "reason to switch" for the growing anti-artificial-sweetener segment.
2. **Built-in function** — a hero line with **3 g creatine monohydrate** (or electrolytes) per bottle. Creatine in RTD is the hottest 2025–26 functional trend and almost absent from chilled grocery shelves.
3. **Swedish-oat plant-based line** — an oat-protein vegan variant leveraging Sweden's oat-drink heritage (Oatly), capturing flexitarians Propud's dairy range can't reach.

Plus a sustainability wrapper (rPET / recyclable mono-material, climate footprint on-pack) that resonates strongly with the Swedish shopper.

**The numbers, in one line.** Estimated landed cost ≈ **SEK 8–10 per bottle**; net selling price to trade ≈ **SEK 13–15**; gross margin ≈ **30–40%**; retail shelf price ≈ **SEK 25–29** single / **~SEK 21/bottle** in 8-packs. Break-even at roughly **1.0–1.3 million bottles/year** against a lean year-1 fixed-cost base.

## 2. Market analysis

### 2.1 Category & tailwinds
- Sweden's **dietary supplements market ≈ USD 1.51 bn (2024)**, growing ~7.6%/yr to 2030; **proteins & amino acids are the fastest-growing segment**.
- Sweden's **liquid dietary supplements ≈ USD 109 m (2024) → USD 182 m (2030), ~9% CAGR.**
- The RTD-protein category globally is compounding low-double-digits; the Nordics over-index on protein/functional snacking.
- Structural drivers: high-protein diets gone mainstream, sugar reduction, "food as function," GLP-1 users seeking protein density, and grab-and-go convenience.

### 2.2 Competitive landscape
| Player | Position | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Propud / NJIE** | Category creator & leader in chilled RTD protein shakes | 330 ml, 20 g protein, lactose-free, no added sugar; huge flavor range ("Craveyard" dessert flavors); strong ICA/Coop/Willys presence. The brand to beat. |
| **Barebells (Vitamin Well Group)** | Premium protein bars + shakes; global | Parent group revenue ≈ €650 m, 40+ markets. Deep pockets, ambient + chilled. |
| **Arla Protein / store brands** | Dairy incumbent + private label | Lower price, less "brand cool," shelf power. |
| **Gainomax, Clean Drink, NOCCO-adjacent** | Sports/functional niches | Various positionings; some ambient. |

**Read:** Propud owns "indulgent dessert flavors + no sugar." Barebells owns "premium global protein." The open, defensible lanes are **clean-label (no artificial sweeteners)**, **added function (creatine/electrolytes)**, and **plant-based oat protein** — none of which the leader fully occupies.

### 2.3 Target customer
- **Core:** 18–40, gym-goers and "active everyday" urbanites who already buy Propud/Barebells but read labels and dislike aspartame.
- **Secondary:** flexitarians/vegans (oat line), and function-seekers (creatine line) currently buying powders.
- **Channel reality:** ~80% of volume will come from chilled grocery multipacks; convenience/petrol and gym/e-com drive single-bottle trial and margin.

## 4. Product & differentiation strategy

### 4.1 Launch range (keep it tight)
1. **Chocolate** (category's #1 flavor)
2. **Vanilla**
3. **Strawberry & cream**
4. **Coffee / cappuccino** (high margin, café tie-in)

Then a fast-follow "dessert" flavor (salted caramel / cookie) once velocity is proven.

### 4.2 The three differentiators (our "reasons to switch")
1. **No artificial sweeteners.** Front-of-pack: *"Sötat bara med stevia — inga konstgjorda sötningsmedel."* Directly attacks Propud's aspartame/ace-K. Cheap to claim, hard for the leader to copy without reformulating its whole range.
2. **Function line — "+Creatine" (3 g) or "+Electrolytes."** Creatine RTD is barely on chilled shelves; this is a genuine innovation moat for 12–18 months and supports a price premium.
3. **Oat-protein vegan line.** Leverages Swedish oat-drink credibility; opens a customer base dairy can't serve. Higher formulation difficulty but strong PR.

### 4.3 Brand & sustainability
- Scandinavian-minimal design, bold flavor cues, clear protein number.
- **rPET bottle / recyclable mono-material**, climate footprint on-pack, Pant (deposit) compliance. Sustainability is a tiebreaker, not the headline.
- Tone: confident, playful, a little less "dessert-gluttony" than Propud — more "clean fuel."

## 5. Cost per bottle (COGS) estimate — the core ask

**Spec modeled:** 330 ml chilled RTD shake, ~20–25 g dairy protein, lactose-free, no added sugar, rPET bottle with sleeve label, contract-manufactured (toll/co-pack), moderate volume (1–3 M bottles/yr).

> Three scenarios: **Low** (high volume / efficient co-packer), **Base** (realistic year 1–2), **High** (small scale / premium spec). The functional-actives line is what the "+Creatine" hero SKU adds; the plain SKU omits it.

| Cost component | Low (SEK) | **Base (SEK)** | High (SEK) | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Milk + milk protein concentrate (~20–25 g protein) | 2.20 | **2.80** | 3.80 | Largest input; tracks dairy/MPC prices |
| Cocoa / flavor system | 0.30 | **0.45** | 0.70 | Chocolate > vanilla |
| Functional actives (3 g creatine *or* electrolytes) | 0.40 | **0.70** | 1.20 | Hero SKU only |
| Sweeteners (stevia / monk fruit) | 0.10 | **0.18** | 0.30 | Stevia pricier than aspartame — part of our premium |
| Stabilizers + lactase enzyme | 0.20 | **0.30** | 0.45 | |
| Primary packaging (rPET bottle, cap, sleeve label) | 1.80 | **2.40** | 3.20 | rPET adds ~5–15% vs virgin |
| Secondary packaging (tray, shrink, carton) | 0.30 | **0.40** | 0.55 | |
| Co-pack tolling (UHT/pasteurise, fill, QC, labor, OH) | 1.50 | **2.20** | 3.50 | Falls hard with volume |
| **Ex-works subtotal** | **6.80** | **9.43** | **13.70** | |
| Inbound freight + cold warehousing + spoilage/waste | 0.60 | **0.90** | 1.40 | Chilled logistics premium |
| **Landed COGS / bottle** | **≈7.40** | **≈10.30** | **≈15.10** | |
| *Same, plain SKU (no functional actives)* | *≈7.00* | *≈9.60* | *≈13.90* | |

**Headline estimate: ≈ SEK 9–10 per bottle landed (base case, functional SKU); ≈ SEK 8–9 for the plain clean-label SKU.** At scale this trends toward SEK 7. Propud, at full scale and dairy-only, likely sits at the low end (~SEK 6–7) — so we must accept a slightly thinner cost position early and earn it back on price/mix from differentiation.

**Sensitivities that matter most:**
- *Dairy/MPC price* — the single biggest swing; hedge or contract.
- *Volume / co-pack utilization* — tolling and packaging per-unit drop sharply above ~3 M bottles/yr.
- *rPET premium and Pant fees* — model explicitly, they're real.
- *Spoilage* — chilled short-dated product punishes over-forecasting.

The differentiators (no artificial sweeteners, +creatine, oat line) justify *parity-to-slight-premium* pricing rather than discounting against the leader — critical, because a price war against Propud's scale is unwinnable.

## 7. Operations & supply chain

- **Model: asset-light, contract-manufactured.** Do **not** build a factory. Partner with a European chilled-dairy / aseptic co-packer that already runs protein RTD.
- **MOQs to expect:** ~1,000–5,000 units/SKU at micro co-packers (higher unit cost); ~1,000–3,000 *cases*/SKU at established PET lines. Plan the first run around a realistic, sellable quantity — over-ordering chilled stock is the classic startup killer.
- **Shelf life:** start chilled (pasteurised). Evaluate **UHT/aseptic ambient** later — it unlocks convenience/petrol and export with far less spoilage risk, at some taste/cost trade-off.
- **Quality & compliance:** EU food law, Livsmedelsverket, nutrition/health-claim rules (creatine claims are regulated — use only authorised wording), allergen labeling, and **Pant deposit registration** for bottles.
- **Logistics:** 3PL with chilled capability; tight demand forecasting and short production cycles to control waste.

# Part B — United States Edition

> *The US RTD-protein market is ~10× Sweden's but dominated by Coca-Cola (Fairlife) and Danone (Premier Protein) competing on price and scale. The Swedish strategy does **not** port over unchanged. This part adapts format, costs (USD), positioning, and go-to-market — and is honest about the degree of difficulty.*

## B1. The single most important strategic truth

In Sweden, "lactose-free + no added sugar + 20 g protein" is differentiated (vs Propud's aspartame). **In the US it is the baseline.** Fairlife already owns ultra-filtered, lactose-free, high-protein, low-calorie dairy, backed by Coca-Cola's distribution. Premier Protein owns cheap-and-everywhere, backed by Danone. **You cannot out-price or out-distribute them.** A US entry only works as a **premium, differentiated niche brand** — DTC/Amazon and specialty/gym first, mass retail much later (if ever). Plan accordingly or don't enter.

## B2. US market (size & momentum)
- US RTD protein shakes ≈ **$1.8 bn (2025)**, growing ~7–8% in value / **~11.5% in volume**; ~**1.25 bn bottles sold in 2024**, projected to ~3.4 bn by 2033.
- North America = ~48% of the global RTD protein market; whey ~61% of product.
- Demand drivers stronger than Sweden: protein obsession, **GLP-1 users** needing protein density, Costco/club-channel volume, creatine going mainstream.

## B3. The competition you're actually up against
| Brand (owner) | Format | Protein / cal | ~Price/bottle | Why it wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fairlife Nutrition Plan** (Coca-Cola) | 11.5 oz | 30 g / 150 cal | **~$1.58** (Costco 18-ct) | Best protein-per-calorie-per-dollar; ultra-filtered; massive distribution |
| **Fairlife Core Power** (Coca-Cola) | 14 / 26 oz | 26–42 g | ~$2.38 (14 oz) | Performance/athlete halo |
| **Premier Protein** (Danone) | 11 oz | 30 g / 160 cal | **~$1.30–1.60** | Cheapest, 14+ flavors, everywhere |
| **Muscle Milk** (PepsiCo) | 11–14 oz | 25–40 g | ~$2.00 | Legacy performance brand |
| **OWYN** (Simply Good Foods) | 12 oz | 20–30 g | ~$2.50–3.00 | **Plant-based, allergen-free** niche leader |
| **Orgain / Koia / others** | 11–14 oz | 20–26 g | ~$2.50–3.50 | Organic / clean / plant niches |

**The open lanes** (where the giants are weak): **(1) no artificial sweeteners** — Fairlife, Premier and Core Power all use sucralose and/or acesulfame-K; **(2) functional RTD (creatine/electrolytes)** — barely present in chilled/ambient grocery; **(3) genuinely premium taste + Scandinavian "clean dessert" brand story** as an imported premium; **(4) clean-label dairy** (OWYN owns clean *plant*, but clean *dairy* is open).

## B4. Product changes required for the US
- **Format:** switch from 330 ml to the US standard **11.5 oz (340 ml)** or **14 oz (414 ml)**. 11.5 oz hits the Fairlife benchmark; 14 oz reads "more value."
- **Protein:** raise to **30 g** — below that you're invisible on shelf next to Fairlife/Premier.
- **Calories:** target ~150–160 cal to match the leaders' "30 g / 150 cal" headline.
- **Sweetener:** stevia/monk-fruit only → front-of-pack **"No artificial sweeteners"** (your sharpest US claim).
- **Packaging:** strongly consider **aseptic / UHT (ambient, shelf-stable)** rather than chilled. Ambient unlocks Amazon/DTC shipping, club, and convenience with minimal spoilage — critical for a startup. (Trade-off: bigger MOQs.)
- **Hero line:** **"+5 g Creatine"** ambient shake — creatine monohydrate has an FDA GRAS notice (GRN 931) for use in foods including milkshakes, so the path exists (see regulatory caveat B8).

## B6. US pricing & unit economics
Two worlds — **pick premium, not mass:**

| | Mass channel (DON'T) | **Premium channel (DO)** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shelf price / bottle | ~$1.79 | **~$2.99–3.49** |
| Channel | Walmart/Costco | DTC/Amazon, Whole Foods, Sprouts, gyms |
| Retailer margin | ~40% | ~40% |
| Distributor margin | ~25% | ~25% (or skip via DTC) |
| Your net ex-works | ~$0.80 | **~$1.55–1.85** (retail) / **~$2.50+** (DTC) |
| Landed COGS | ~$1.16 | ~$1.16 |
| **Gross margin/bottle** | **negative 😵** | **~30–40% retail; 55%+ DTC** |

**Takeaway:** DTC/Amazon and specialty retail are the only viable starting points — the margin there funds the brand. Mass retail at $1.79 loses money against your cost base; enter it only after scale drives COGS down *and* the brand commands a premium endcap.

## B7. US go-to-market
**Phase 1 — DTC + Amazon + gyms (months 0–9).** Launch ambient 30 g shakes (chocolate, vanilla, coffee) and the **+creatine** hero on your own site, Amazon, and through gym/studio accounts. Subscription model on DTC (recurring revenue, fat margins). Influencer/UGC around "no artificial sweeteners" and "creatine you can drink." This proves rate-of-sale cheaply, no slotting fees.

**Phase 2 — specialty/natural retail (months 9–24).** With DTC velocity as proof, sell into **Whole Foods, Sprouts, Erewhon, regional natural chains, and convenience** via a regional DSD or broker. Budget for **slotting fees ($10k–50k+ per chain)** and demos — US retail is pay-to-play.

**Phase 3 — selective mass + club (24 months+).** Only once COGS is at scale and brand pull exists. Costco club rotation is a volume jackpot but punishing on margin and terms.

**Brand:** lean into the **Scandinavian-import premium** angle — clean, design-forward, "European clean-label dessert protein." It's a genuine differentiator in a sea of American sports-nutrition branding. (Note: the "VÄLT" name is a placeholder; for the US, test names that read well in English.)

## B8. US regulatory & compliance (different from Sweden — get counsel)
- **FDA**, not Livsmedelsverket. Conventional-food **Nutrition Facts** panel, ingredient/allergen labeling (milk allergen), and **cGMP** at the co-packer.
- **Creatine caveat (important):** creatine has a **GRAS notice for use as a food ingredient** (GRN 931), which supports putting it in a *conventional beverage*. But the line between "conventional food" and "dietary supplement" matters: structure/function claims ("supports muscle") may pull you toward **supplement** classification (DSHEA, 21 CFR 111, required disclaimer). **Get FDA regulatory counsel before finalizing the creatine SKU and its claims** — this is the #1 compliance risk.
- **California Prop 65** warnings; **state bottle-deposit** laws (CA, MI, OR, NY, etc. — no national system like Sweden's Pant); **state cottage/processing licensing**.
- Health claims are tightly policed by FDA/FTC — substantiate everything.

| Gross margin | ~45% (DTC-heavy) | ~38% | ~34% |

- **US break-even is later and costlier than Sweden** — marketing/CAC and slotting are the killers. Expect **$3–6 M+ to reach profitability**, vs SEK 4–8 M for Sweden.
- The aseptic MOQ + US CAC mean **don't go to the US under-capitalized.** A focused DTC/Amazon launch is the capital-efficient wedge.

## B10. Honest recommendation
The US is winnable **only** as a sharply differentiated premium brand entering through DTC/Amazon/gyms — never as a cheaper Fairlife. Your three real weapons are **no artificial sweeteners**, **drinkable creatine**, and a **premium Scandinavian-clean brand**. If the budget or appetite for a $3–6 M, multi-year, marketing-heavy fight isn't there, **launch and prove the model in Sweden/Nordics first (Sections 1–11), then use that traction and unit-cost base to enter the US** — a lower-risk sequence than a cold US start.
