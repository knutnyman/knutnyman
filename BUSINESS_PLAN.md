# US Business Plan — Premium RTD Protein Shake
### A Propud-inspired, US-built ready-to-drink protein shake (working name: **"VÄLT"** — placeholder)

> *Working document. All financial figures are good-faith planning estimates, not audited quotes — validate every cost with real co-packer/supplier quotes before committing capital. Currency: USD. Market: United States (primary and sole focus). Date: 2026.*
>
> **De-risking note:** A lower-capital alternative is to prove the model in Sweden/Nordics first, then enter the US with a proven cost base. This plan assumes you've chosen the direct US route for the larger TAM; the trade-off is more capital and tougher competition up front.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The opportunity.** The US ready-to-drink (RTD) protein category is ~**$1.8B (2025)**, growing ~**7–8%/yr in value and ~11.5%/yr in volume** (≈1.25B bottles in 2024 → ~3.4B by 2033). Demand is structural: mainstream high-protein diets, sugar reduction, GLP-1 users needing protein density, and grab-and-go convenience. The Swedish brand **Propud (NJIE)** proved a winning product formula (great-tasting, no-added-sugar, high-protein dessert shakes); we adapt that formula for US format, taste, and distribution.

**The hard truth that shapes everything.** The US shelf is dominated by **Fairlife (Coca-Cola)** and **Premier Protein (Danone)** competing on price and scale. "Lactose-free, no added sugar, 30 g protein" is **table stakes**, not a differentiator. We therefore enter as a **premium, differentiated niche brand through DTC/Amazon and specialty/gym channels first** — never as a cheaper Fairlife.

**Our three weapons (the reasons to switch):**
1. **No artificial sweeteners** — stevia/monk-fruit only. Fairlife, Premier, and Core Power all use sucralose and/or acesulfame-K. This is our sharpest, cheapest-to-claim differentiator.
2. **Drinkable creatine** — a hero line with **5 g creatine monohydrate** per bottle. Creatine is the hottest 2025–26 functional trend and nearly absent from chilled/ambient grocery RTD.
3. **Premium Scandinavian "clean-dessert" brand** — design-forward, indulgent-but-clean taste and story, distinct in a sea of American sports-nutrition branding.

**The numbers in one line.** Landed cost ≈ **$1.00–1.20/bottle** (startup scale) → ~$0.80 at volume; premium shelf **$2.99–3.49**; gross margin **~30–40% at retail, 55%+ on DTC**. Path to break-even needs **~$3–6M** and 2–3 years, with a capital-efficient DTC/Amazon wedge up front.

## 2. Market & TAM / SAM / SOM

### 2.1 Sizing
| Layer | Definition | Size | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | All US RTD protein shakes | **~$1.8B (2025)**, → ~$3.8B by 2035 | Whole category; ~1.25B bottles/yr |
| **SAM** | Premium + clean-label + functional RTD (the segment we can credibly serve) | **~$350–500M** (~20–28% of TAM) | Premium/natural/functional tiers, growing faster than mass |
| **SOM (5-yr target)** | Realistic capturable share | **~$15–30M revenue** (~3–6% of SAM) | Achievable for a focused challenger brand |

### 2.2 Why now
- **Protein is the macro of the decade** — fortification is spreading across every aisle.
- **GLP-1 tailwind** — millions of users instructed to prioritize protein and lose appetite for solid food; RTD shakes are an ideal vehicle.
- **Anti-artificial-sweetener sentiment** rising (RFK/MAHA-era label scrutiny, "no seed oils / no sucralose" discourse) — directly favors our clean-label claim.
- **Creatine mainstreaming** — moving from gym-bro powder to general-wellness daily habit (women's health, cognition, healthy aging).

### 2.3 Target customer
- **Primary — "Clean Optimizers" (25–45):** already buy Fairlife/Premier or powders, read labels, will pay a premium to avoid sucralose. Gym/studio regulars, runners, biohacker-adjacent.
- **Secondary — "Function Seekers":** want creatine but won't mix powder; convenience converts them.
- **Tertiary — "GLP-1 protein-maxers":** need easy, low-volume, high-protein nutrition.

## 4. Product

### 4.1 Spec (US-tuned)
| Attribute | Spec | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Format | **11.5 oz (340 ml)** core; 14 oz value option later | Match the Fairlife benchmark consumers anchor on |
| Protein | **30 g** (ultra-filtered milk + milk protein isolate) | Below 30 g = invisible on shelf |
| Calories | **~150–160** | Match leaders' "30g/150cal" headline |
| Sugar | No added sugar; lactose-free (lactase) | Table stakes |
| Sweetener | **Stevia / monk fruit only** | The differentiator — "no artificial sweeteners" |
| Fat | Low (≤2.5 g) | Lean positioning |
| Packaging | **Aseptic / UHT (shelf-stable, ambient)** | Enables Amazon/DTC shipping, club, convenience; low spoilage |
| Hero line | **+5 g creatine monohydrate** | Genuine innovation moat |

### 4.2 Launch range (tight)
1. **Chocolate** (#1 category flavor) 2. **Vanilla** 3. **Coffee/Cold Brew** (premium, high-margin, café-culture tie-in)
- Plus the **+Creatine** hero in Chocolate & Vanilla.
- Fast-follow once velocity proven: a "dessert" flavor (salted caramel / cookies & cream) and a 14 oz value size.

### 4.3 Why aseptic/ambient (key ops choice)
Shelf-stable means no cold chain → ships cheaply via Amazon/DTC, survives club/convenience, and slashes spoilage risk. The trade-off is **larger MOQs** (see §7) and a formulation that holds taste through UHT — invest in flavor R&D.

## 6. Cost per bottle — US COGS model (USD, 11.5–14 oz, 30 g, aseptic, co-packed)

| Component | Low (scale) | **Base (startup)** | High (small run) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ultra-filtered milk / milk protein isolate (30 g) | 0.30 | **0.36** | 0.48 |
| Cocoa / flavor system | 0.04 | **0.06** | 0.09 |
| Functional actives (5 g creatine) — hero SKU only | 0.06 | **0.09** | 0.15 |
| Sweetener (stevia / monk fruit) | 0.015 | **0.025** | 0.04 |
| Stabilizers / emulsifiers | 0.02 | **0.03** | 0.05 |
| Primary packaging (bottle/carton, cap, label) | 0.18 | **0.24** | 0.34 |
| Secondary packaging (tray/case) | 0.03 | **0.04** | 0.06 |
| Co-pack tolling (aseptic process, fill, QC, labor, OH) | 0.15 | **0.22** | 0.38 |
| **Ex-works subtotal** | **~0.78** | **~1.07** | **~1.59** |
| Freight + warehousing + spoilage (ambient = low) | 0.06 | **0.09** | 0.16 |
| **Landed COGS / bottle** | **~$0.84** | **~$1.16** | **~$1.75** |
| *Plain SKU (no creatine)* | *~$0.78* | *~$1.07* | *~$1.60* |

**Headline: ≈ $1.00–1.20/bottle landed at startup scale, → ~$0.80 with volume.** Fairlife/Premier at full scale are likely ~$0.55–0.75 — you will not match them, which is exactly why the strategy is premium, not price.

**Biggest cost levers:** (1) dairy/milk-protein price (largest input — contract/hedge); (2) co-pack volume utilization (tolling + packaging drop sharply with scale); (3) creatine dosage on the hero SKU; (4) freight (ambient helps a lot).

## 7. Operations & supply chain

- **Asset-light, contract-manufactured.** Do not build a plant. Partner with a US **aseptic dairy co-packer** experienced in protein RTD (Tetra/UHT lines).
- **MOQ reality (plan capital around this):** aseptic co-packers commonly require **~300k–400k units/run and ~1.5M units/yr minimum** → a first run can be **~$300k–500k** in product alone. If that's too big to start, use a smaller **chilled/ESL** co-packer for a pilot at higher unit cost, prove velocity, then move to aseptic for scale.
- **Second source:** qualify a backup co-packer early — single-supplier dependency is an existential risk.
- **Logistics:** ambient shelf-stable product → standard (non-cold) 3PL; integrate with Amazon FBA and a DTC fulfillment partner.
- **Quality:** cGMP, COAs on every lot (especially creatine identity/purity), allergen (milk) controls.

## 8. Regulatory & compliance (US — get counsel before launch)

- **FDA conventional-food** path: Nutrition Facts panel, ingredient/allergen labeling (milk), cGMP at co-packer.
- **Creatine — the #1 risk to resolve.** Creatine monohydrate has an **FDA GRAS notice (GRN 931)** covering use in foods including milkshakes, so a conventional-beverage path exists. **But** structure/function claims ("supports muscle/strength") can pull the product toward **dietary-supplement** classification (DSHEA, 21 CFR 111, required disclaimer panel). Decide *conventional food vs. supplement* deliberately with an FDA regulatory attorney **before** finalizing the hero SKU label and claims.
- **State rules:** California **Prop 65**; **state bottle-deposit** laws (CA, MI, OR, NY, CT, etc. — no national system); state food-facility licensing.
- **Claims policing:** FDA/FTC substantiation required for all health/structure-function claims — keep evidence files.

## 9. Go-to-market

**Phase 1 — DTC + Amazon + gyms (months 0–9). The capital-efficient wedge.**
- Launch ambient 30 g shakes + the **+Creatine** hero on your **own Shopify site** and **Amazon**, plus **gym/studio** wholesale.
- **Subscription** on DTC = recurring revenue + fat margins. Influencer/UGC engine around "no sucralose" and "creatine you can drink."
- Goal: prove rate-of-sale and nail unit economics with **no slotting fees**.

**Phase 2 — specialty / natural retail (months 9–24).**
- With DTC velocity as proof, sell into **Sprouts, Whole Foods, Erewhon, Thrive, regional natural chains, and premium convenience** via brokers/regional DSD.
- Budget **slotting fees ($10k–50k+/chain)** and demo spend — US retail is pay-to-play.

**Phase 3 — selective mass & club (24 months+).**
- Only after COGS scales and brand pull exists. Costco rotations = volume jackpot but brutal margin/terms. Enter on your terms, not out of desperation.

### 9.1 DTC / Amazon unit economics (illustrative)
| Metric | Assumption |
|---|---|
| DTC price (12-pack) | ~$39 (~$3.25/bottle) |
| COGS + fulfillment per pack | ~$18 (12 × ~$1.16 + ship/pack) |
| Gross margin per pack | ~$21 (~54%) |
| Target blended CAC | **< $35** (recoup on first order + early subscription) |
| LTV lever | Subscription retention — aim 3–4 repeat cycles → LTV $120–160 |
| **Rule** | Keep **LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1**; if CAC > first-order margin, lean harder on subscription + referral, not paid scaling |

CAC discipline is the make-or-break of the US plan — paid social for RTD beverages is expensive, so weight toward **content/UGC, influencer seeding, sampling, and referral** over pure performance spend.

## 11. Risks & mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Coca-Cola/Danone price & shelf power** | Don't fight on price/mass; win on clean-label + creatine + premium brand; DTC-first to avoid their shelf. |
| **High US CAC erodes economics** | UGC/influencer/sampling over paid; subscription + referral; enforce LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 gate before scaling spend. |
| **Aseptic MOQ / capital intensity** | Pilot via chilled/ESL co-packer first; raise enough for one full aseptic run; pre-sell DTC to de-risk inventory. |
| **Creatine regulatory classification** | FDA counsel up front; choose food vs. supplement deliberately; compliant claims only. |
| **Dairy price volatility** | Contract/hedge milk-protein inputs. |
| **Slotting fees before brand pull** | Prove DTC/gym velocity first to negotiate from strength; start regional. |
| **Taste loss through UHT** | Invest in flavor R&D; blind-test vs Fairlife/Premier before launch — taste drives repeat. |
| **Co-packer dependency** | Qualify a second source early. |
